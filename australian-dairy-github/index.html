Chapter 2 The farms

The farms are fewer. The picture is bigger.

Australia had 3,772 registered dairy farms in 2024–25, down from 6,770 in 2011–12. Output has not fallen at the same pace: farm counts alone do not describe the industry's capacity.

Fewer farms.
A broader story.

Farm numbers, milk output and demand do not move together. Australia's dairy story combines fewer registered farms, production concentrated in a few states, changing retail habits and a wide range of products sold overseas. Each measure reveals a different part of that change.

Sources & reading notes

Data checked 13 October 2026

Milk production

Dairy Australia, Production & Sales Statistics. The latest six-year workbook supplies the revised observations shown for 2020–21 to 2025–26. The current YTD workbook supplies July–August 2026 only.

Revisions matter: the current 2024–25 national total is 8,525.2 million litres; the earlier report stated 8,315 million litres. Production trends use the revised values. The state-profile comparison preserves the original report's internally consistent farm, cow and production snapshot.

Production workbook · Latest YTD workbook

Farms, milk uses and exports

Dairy Australia, Australian Dairy Industry In Focus 2025. Registered farms: Table 2; cows: Table 3; production: Table 6; milk allocation: Figure 9; milk sales: Table 11; export products and markets: Tables 8–9. The report credits dairy manufacturers, state authorities and ABS where applicable.

Latest farm counts are provisional and cow counts estimated. Cow counts are rounded and labelled at 31 March. “Cows per farm” divides that count by annual registered farms; it is an approximate structural ratio. Farm counts do not identify why numbers changed.

Product quantities, raw-milk litres, retail purchases and export dollars measure different things. They are kept in separate comparisons.

An independent view of demand

ABS, Apparent Consumption of Selected Foodstuffs, Australia, 2023–24, Table 1.1, released 28 March 2025. This was the latest release available at the date checked. All comparison years use that release's revised series.

Total purchases are measured in thousand tonnes; the per-person measure is grams per day. Percentage changes compare each with its own 2018–19 starting value. Dairy milk includes cow, sheep and goat milk. Unflavoured milk alternatives are shown separately.

These retail-purchase estimates exclude non-retail channels such as restaurants, do not deduct waste, and are not measured dietary intake. They are not combined with Dairy Australia's broader consumption estimates. ABS copyright and attribution.

Maps, measures and design

State boundaries: ABS ASGS 2021 generalised state/territory boundaries. World context: Natural Earth public-domain geography via Vega Datasets. Locations used for state symbols and export links are representative anchors.

Australian maps use an Australia-centred equal-area projection; the export map uses a Mercator projection focused on the Asia-Pacific region. Missing territory data are distinct from zero. Percent change means (end ÷ start − 1) × 100. Financial years run July–June. Small discrepancies in report tables can result from source rounding.
